# Style DNA - read from the nine portfolio pieces

Files are in `assets/portfolio/` (1-9). Look at them; this text is a reading aid, not a substitute.

## What all nine share
- **Square, centred hero.** Subject fills 55-75% of frame height, on or near the vertical centre line.
- **Dark, saturated world.** Backgrounds sit in low-to-mid values; the subject carries the highlights.
- **Two hues + one accent.** Purple/orange (1), navy/fire with cyan accent (2), amber/navy (3),
  blue/violet with cyan-magenta accent (4), black/red with yellow-orange fire (5), black/violet with
  white (6), red/violet with blue eyes (7), yellow-green vs crimson split (8), night blue with warm
  window lights and a red accent (9).
- **Glow as the accent.** Something emits light: pumpkin, visor, lanterns, moon, flames, stars,
  lightning. Glow colour = accent colour, and it rim-lights the subject.
- **Clean cel shading with soft gradients.** Confident outlines, 2-3 shade steps, airbrushed bounce
  light, crisp specular hits on eyes, metal, leather.
- **Depth in three layers.** Foreground framing (leaves, rubble, chairs, rocks), midground subject,
  far background (forest, city, sky, moon). Foreground is darkest.
- **Particles.** Falling leaves, embers, sparkles, stars, lightning arcs - small, scattered, on-palette.
- **Title is designed, not typed.** Arched or wide-tracked, gradient fill, stroke and glow matched to
  the accent, placed top (1, 2, 5) or bottom (7). Four of nine have no title at all.

## Composition rules
- Head inside the central 70% circle; never crop a chin, ear tip or hat brim with the circle edge.
- Title zone: top 8-22% or bottom 78-92% of height. Keep that band low-detail in the base art.
- Eye-line at 35-45% of height for portraits, 30-38% for full body.
- One diagonal for energy: coat sweep, tail curve, sword, light beam.
- Faces look at the viewer or at each other; avoid profile unless the brief demands it.

## Lighting recipe
1. Key light from above-front, soft, slightly warm or neutral.
2. Rim light from behind in the accent colour, thin and bright.
3. Emissive prop or element near the subject for a colour pop on skin or clothes.
4. Background fog or haze behind the head so the silhouette separates.

## What makes a piece look cheap
- Three or more competing accent colours.
- Background as sharp and bright as the subject.
- Default system font, no stroke, no glow, centred mid-frame over the face.
- Generator text left in the image; mangled hands; asymmetrical eyes.
- Over-sharpened upscales with crunchy halos; heavy grain on skin.
